When can the usual metrics be trusted?

Study 02a hides the assignment label of the 01a experiment and compares exposure-based metrics with the randomized benchmark. Studies 02b and 02d use a separate Criteo display log, from credit rules to bidder choices. Study 02c uses brand spending to test when channel returns are measurable.

02a

Can an exposure-based attribution metric be trusted as a measure of ad impact?

Exposure-based attribution counted 23,031 conversions in the group eligible for ads; the experiment estimates 13,687 additional conversions (95% CI 12,887–14,488), so the count is 68% higher. Covariate adjustment moved the observed visit-rate difference between exposed and unexposed users from 38.0 pp to 24.6–25.5 pp, between the two experimental point estimates.

02b

When a user sees several ads before converting, how much does the credit rule change which touch gets credit?

65% of converting paths have several touches, and last touch gives no credit to 82% of the touches in them. On these paths, a time-decay rule fitted to observed touch-to-conversion delays moves last-touch credit from 100% to 39%. These rules split credit; they do not show which touches caused the conversion.

02c

Can a marketing mix model recover each channel's return from a brand's own spend and revenue, and can one tell in advance when it will?

In simulations built on each brand's observed spend, a marketing mix model recovered a channel's planted return more accurately where that channel's spend varied on its own: median error 19% for 33 brand-channels with a signal-to-noise index of 2 or more and 50% for the other 21 (daily data). 19% of weekly and 55% of daily brand-channels reach that index. On observed spend, a return of 2 would be detectable for 6% of weekly and 42% of daily brand-channels.

02d

If a bidder values impressions by expected credit, does the attribution rule change what it buys?

Hardly, in this log. At the primary budget (50% of logged cost) the two bidders overlap on 90.4% of cost, and cross-rule credit loss is −0.2% under last-touch scoring and 2.2% under time-decay scoring, below the pre-specified 5%. This is a replay at fixed logged prices, not an online experiment. The time-decay bidder puts a smaller share of its budget on impressions within 24 hours of a click (19.9% vs 24.9%).
