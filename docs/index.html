The ads worked. Proving how much is the hard part.

In a randomized experiment on 14 million users, advertising raised the conversion rate by 59%. A simple attribution count would have credited it with 68% more conversions than it caused. 1 · With an experiment

The ads were effective: visits rose 27% and conversions 59%.

Users were assigned at random, so any difference between the two groups was caused by the ads.

Visit and conversion rates by group

Each row moves from the control group's rate to the ad group's rate. With 14 million users, the 95% confidence intervals are narrower than the dots.

2 · Without an experiment

Without an experiment, the credit depends on the attribution rule.

The simplest rule credits the ads with every conversion by a user who was shown one. The experiment shows how many of those the ads caused.

Outcomes credited by the simple count and caused by the ads

Ad group only. Caused = the experiment's estimate of outcomes the ads added, with its 95% confidence interval. Right: of every 100 outcomes the count credits to the ads, how many the ads caused.

Conversion credit by attribution rule

Does the rule change what a bidder buys?

Two bidders replay the same logged impressions, one trained on each rule's credit.

Budget

Budget is a share of logged spend. Spend overlap: share of spend on the same impressions. Credit lost: how much less credit a bidder captures, scored by one rule, when it was trained on the other rule; 95% intervals.

3 · Planning a test

Measuring the effect again requires a test, and a large one for conversions.

Conversions are about twenty times rarer than visits, so a test of conversions needs about twenty times the users.
